The game

Help with the keyboard

Where is it? always lights up the key and says where it is on the keyboard.

Hiding them leaves fewer keys on the screen, which also means fewer steps for switch users.

For students with tremors: after a key press, other presses are ignored for a moment. Holding a key down never counts as more than one press.

Settings

Says which key to find. Screen reader users hear the same words from VoiceOver or ChromeVox, so you can turn this off for them.

Plays the teacher's own recordings (the letters A to Z, Find, Try again, Type and The monster is full!). Anything not recorded uses the computer voice below.

Voices come from the device, so each iPad or Chromebook has its own list. For a more natural voice on an iPad: Settings, Accessibility, Spoken Content, Voices, English, then download a voice marked Enhanced or Premium (for example Ava or Zoe). Close and reopen the game, and it picks that voice by itself. On a Chromebook, the Google voices sound the most natural and are picked by themselves when the Chromebook is online.

Off: calm mode. No glowing, wobbling or flying letters.

On: tapping the big letter opens the iPad or tablet keyboard. Off: use the big keys in the game, or a real keyboard.

Play full screen

This browser cannot hide its own address bar, so the game has hidden its buttons instead. Use Exit full screen in the corner to get them back.

For true full screen on an iPhone or iPad, add the game to the Home Screen once. It then opens full screen every time.

  1. In Safari, tap the Share button (the square with an arrow). On a newer iPhone it may be under the ⋯ button.
  2. Tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  3. Open Monster Keys from the Home Screen.

For a locked-down lesson, turn on Guided Access in iPad Settings under Accessibility, then triple-click the top or Home button inside the game.
